My research focuses on developing scalable optimization and AI methods for complex engineering and operational systems. A recurring motivation is the gap between the quality of decisions produced by mathematical optimization and the computational time available in real-time operations.

## Agentic AI for adaptive decomposition and coordination

Large optimization problems often need to be decomposed into smaller subproblems. I am interested in AI systems that reason about decision-variable and constraint dependencies, identify sources of computational difficulty, and adapt decomposition and coordination strategies as a solver progresses. The goal is to move beyond manually specified partitions and coordination rules toward systems that learn how to organize and coordinate optimization at scale.

## AI-accelerated combinatorial optimization

I study ways to combine mathematical programming with machine learning so that optimization algorithms can produce strong decisions faster, while preserving feasibility and the structure of the original problem.

## Optimization for transportation and infrastructure

My doctoral research developed network-level traffic signal control methods for connected and automated transportation systems. This work motivates broader research on coordinating decisions across interconnected infrastructure components.

## Optimization for robotics and automation

My industry research addresses optimization problems arising in large-scale robotic fulfillment and automated operations. Public project descriptions and publications will be added as they are ready for release.
